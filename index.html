Logica 2.1 – Serial Edition

Patches your original Logica THE DIAGNOSTIC 2.0 ROMs with a serial and parallel console: every screen and menu also on a terminal, so you can diagnose an Amiga with no working video.

After patching you get:

  • ROM files for an emulator, or for burning to EPROM (A500/A600/A2000 and A1200/A4000).
  • A floppy disk (ADF) of Logica that boots like any other floppy, for an Amiga without a Logica EPROM.

Everything happens in your browser; your files are never uploaded. Patch ….

Drop your Logica 2.0 dump here: either the hi and the lo file together (32-bit), in any order, or one 512 KB file (an emulator or 16-bit image). Both work as read or byte-swapped.

What the patch does

  • A text console on the parallel port from the very first instruction, before any RAM is tested: one line per boot step, then every screen Logica draws (VT100/ANSI, 80×25).
  • The same console on the serial port, for a board whose CIAs are dead.
  • The menus and tests are driven from the terminal's keyboard. Logica's tests themselves are unchanged and give the same verdicts as 2.0.
  • The whole boot log stays on the terminal, followed by a summary of the CPU, FPU and RAM found.
  • The power LED blinks during boot, a sign of life even without a terminal. The old step LEDs are gone.
  • The title, About pages and version say THE DIAGNOSTIC 2.1, with "Serial goes Parallel" below.
  • Logica 2.0's memory ADDRESS ERROR REPORT bug is fixed: the failing chip is marked on the right block.

Build adapters

The console needs a way from the Amiga's port to a USB port on your computer.

Parallel port cable (simplest)

Use a 5 V (or 5 V tolerant 3.3 V) USB-TTL adapter, never an RS-232 one, and a DB25 male plug:

Pin 11 (BUSY)to the adapter's RX
Pins 10 and 12tied together, wired straight to the adapter's TX (no resistor)
Pins 18–25to the adapter's GND

Serial port

An ordinary USB to RS-232 adapter with a null-modem cable to the Amiga's DB25 serial port.

USB-C adapter boards

Small boards that plug straight into the parallel or the serial port, with USB-C on the other end, are being tested. Their files will be published here once the first ones are built and work.

Why the image is repeated

Every Amiga ROM chip is 16 bits wide. A 27C400 holds 512 KB, which is all the Amiga's ROM area asks for. A 27C800 or a 27C160 / MX29F1615 has one or two address lines more (A18, A19), which the Amiga does not use to pick anything: depending on the board they are tied high, tied low, set by a jumper or left free. So the image is written again and again until the chip is full; whatever those lines do, the chip answers with the same ROM.

  • A500 / A600 / A2000: the image once per 512 KB (a 256 KB Kickstart 1.x twice per 512 KB): 1 copy on a 27C400, 2 on a 27C800, 4 on a 27C160 / MX29F1615.
  • A1200 / A4000: the image is split into its hi and lo halves of 256 KB each, and each half is repeated: 2 copies per chip on a 27C400, 4 on a 27C800, 8 on a 27C160 / MX29F1615.

This only fits the image to the chip; the ROM itself must suit the model (a Kickstart made for the A1200 will not boot an A500). A 40-pin chip in a 42-pin socket leaves the socket's two end pins free; check the board for which end. A 42-pin chip in a 40-pin socket needs an adapter that ties its extra address pins. The MX29F1615 needs a programmer (or adapter) that supports it.

Tested: Logica 2.1, the byte-swapped 16-bit file on an MX29F1615, boots an A500+ (rev 8 board). The chip goes straight into all 42 holes of the socket, notch to notch; the original 40-pin Kickstart sits one row away from the notch.
